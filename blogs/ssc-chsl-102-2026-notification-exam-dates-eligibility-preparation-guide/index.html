SSC CHSL (10+2) 2026: Your Gateway to Government Careers – Notification & Exam Details

Aspirants eyeing a career in government ministries and departments through the Combined Higher Secondary Level (CHSL) Examination will be keen to mark their calendars! The Staff Selection Commission (SSC) is gearing up to release the official notification for the CHSL (10+2) 2026 examination, promising a fresh wave of opportunities for 12th-pass candidates across the nation.

While some might have anticipated a March 31st notification, the latest updates indicate that the SSC CHSL 2026 notification is officially slated for release on April 30, 2026. The application window will then open from April 30 to May 31, 2026. Prospective candidates should note that the Tier-I examination is scheduled to take place between July and September 2026. This comprehensive guide will walk you through all the crucial details you need to know to prepare effectively.

Important Dates at a Glance

Staying informed about key dates is paramount for any competitive exam. Here's a quick overview of the essential timelines for SSC CHSL 2026:

✅ Official Notification Release: April 30, 2026

✅ Online Application Start Date: April 30, 2026

✅ Online Application Last Date: May 31, 2026

✅ Tier-I Examination Window: July - September 2026

✅ Tier-II Examination: Expected after Tier-I results (Tentatively December 2026 or January 2027)

Eligibility Criteria for SSC CHSL 2026

Before applying, candidates must ensure they meet the eligibility requirements set by the Staff Selection Commission. The criteria generally cover age limit, educational qualification, and nationality.

1. Age Limit
Candidates must typically be between 18 and 27 years of age as of the cut-off date mentioned in the official notification. Age relaxations are applicable for candidates belonging to reserved categories (SC/ST, OBC, PwBD, Ex-Servicemen) as per government norms.
2. Educational Qualification
Aspirants must have passed Class 12th (10+2) or an equivalent examination from a recognized Board or University. For specific posts like Data Entry Operator (DEO) Grade 'A' in certain Ministries/Departments, candidates are required to have passed Class 12th in the Science stream with Mathematics as a subject. Candidates appearing for their 12th-grade examinations can also apply, but they must possess the essential qualification by the specified cut-off date.
3. Nationality
Applicants must be either a citizen of India, a subject of Nepal or Bhutan, a Tibetan refugee, or a person of Indian origin who has migrated from certain countries with the intention of permanently settling in India.

SSC CHSL 2026 Exam Pattern

The selection process for SSC CHSL 2026 comprises two tiers: Tier-I and Tier-II, both conducted as Computer-Based Tests (CBT).

Tier-I Examination

Tier-I is an objective-type online exam designed to screen candidates. It consists of 100 questions across four sections, carrying a total of 200 marks. The duration of the exam is 60 minutes (80 minutes for PwD candidates). There is a negative marking of 0.50 marks for each incorrect answer.

Key Aspects of Tier-I:

✅ Objective Type, Multiple Choice Questions

✅ Online Mode (Computer-Based)

✅ Total 100 Questions, 200 Marks

✅ Duration: 60 Minutes (80 minutes for PwD candidates)

✅ Negative Marking: 0.50 marks for each wrong answer

Tier-II Examination

Candidates who qualify Tier-I will be eligible to appear for Tier-II. The Tier-II exam has been revised and consists of two sessions, typically conducted on the same day. Session-I includes objective-type multiple-choice questions across different sections, while Session-II comprises a Skill Test/Typing Test.

Structure of Tier-II (as per latest pattern):

The Tier-II exam is divided into two sessions on the same day.

Session-I: Main Examination (Objective Type)

  • Section I: Module-I (Mathematical Abilities) and Module-II (Reasoning & General Intelligence)
  • Section II: Module-I (English Language & Comprehension) and Module-II (General Awareness)
  • Section III: Module-I (Computer Knowledge Test)

Session-II: Skill Test/Typing Test

  • Module-II of Section III (Skill Test/Typing Test)

There is a negative marking of 1 mark for every wrong answer in Sections I, II, and Module I of Section III in Tier-II.

Anticipated Vacancies

The Staff Selection Commission will announce the tentative number of vacancies for various posts such as Lower Division Clerk (LDC), Junior Secretariat Assistant (JSA), Postal Assistant (PA), Sorting Assistant (SA), and Data Entry Operator (DEO) along with the official notification on April 30, 2026. For reference, 3,131 vacancies were announced in 2025.

Preparation Strategy for CHSL 2026

With the notification approaching, it's time to refine your preparation strategy. Here are some key tips:

1. Understand the Syllabus & Exam Pattern
Thoroughly go through the detailed syllabus for both Tier-I and Tier-II, including subjects like English Language, General Intelligence, Quantitative Aptitude, General Awareness, and Computer Knowledge. Understand the marking scheme and negative marking.
2. Create a Study Plan
Develop a realistic and structured study plan that covers all subjects, allocating sufficient time for each topic. Consistent study is key to success.
3. Practice with Mock Tests
Regularly take mock tests to familiarize yourself with the exam environment, improve time management, and identify your strengths and weaknesses. Analyze your performance to work on areas needing improvement.
4. Focus on Accuracy and Speed
Given the negative marking in both tiers, prioritize accuracy. Simultaneously, practice to increase your speed, especially for the time-bound Tier-I and the typing/skill test in Tier-II.

Stay Updated!

The release of the SSC CHSL 2026 notification on April 30, 2026, marks the beginning of an important recruitment cycle. Keep a close watch on the official SSC website (ssc.gov.in) for the detailed notification and all subsequent updates. Start your preparation early, stay focused, and aim for success in this highly competitive examination!
