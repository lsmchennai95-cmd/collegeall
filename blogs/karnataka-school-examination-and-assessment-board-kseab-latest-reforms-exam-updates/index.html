Navigating Educational Excellence: Latest Reforms from the Karnataka School Examination and Assessment Board (KSEAB)

The landscape of education is ever-evolving, and at the forefront of this transformation in Karnataka stands the Karnataka School Examination and Assessment Board (KSEAB). Established in 1966 as the Secondary Education Examination Board, it merged with the Department of Pre-University Education (DPUE) in 2022 to form the comprehensive KSEAB we know today. This pivotal body is responsible for shaping the academic journey of millions of students from Class 6 to Class 12, ensuring quality education and standardized evaluation across the state.

The Core Mandate of KSEAB

KSEAB's responsibilities extend far beyond just conducting examinations. It plays a multi-faceted role in maintaining and elevating educational standards in Karnataka.

✅ Curriculum Development: Prescribing and periodically reviewing the syllabus and curriculum for secondary (Class 10) and higher secondary (Class 12) classes.

✅ Textbook & Study Material Approval: Approving and publishing essential textbooks and study materials for affiliated schools.

✅ Examination & Result Management: Conducting the crucial SSLC (Class 10) and 2nd PUC (Class 12) board examinations and ensuring timely declaration of results.

✅ School Affiliation & Standards: Recognizing and affiliating schools under its jurisdiction and upholding transparency and academic standards.

✅ Online Services: Offering convenient online services for form filling, result checking, and certificate verification.

Pioneering Reforms for Student-Centric Learning

In a significant move to reduce academic pressure and foster holistic learning, KSEAB has introduced several student-friendly reforms, particularly for the ongoing and upcoming academic years.

1. Introduction of Three Annual Examinations for 2nd PUC

Breaking away from the traditional supplementary exam system, KSEAB has implemented three annual examinations (Exam 1, Exam 2, and Exam 3) for 2nd PUC students. This innovative approach allows students multiple opportunities to improve their scores, with the best performance among the three attempts being considered for the final result.

2. Reduced Pass Marks and SSLC Evaluation Changes

To align with national standards like CBSE and ICSE, KSEAB has approved a reduction in the minimum pass marks for SSLC and 2nd PUC exams from 35% to 33% starting from the 2025-26 academic year. Furthermore, for SSLC students, the total marks will now be calculated out of 525, down from 625. This change comes with the exclusion of third language marks from the final total. While the third language examination remains compulsory, students will now receive grades (A, B, C, D) instead of marks, aiming to reduce pressure.

3. Introduction of Moral Science

From the current academic year (2025-26), Moral Science has been introduced as a new subject, with the government providing free textbooks to instill ethical values and promote social responsibility among students.

Understanding Passing Criteria

For the 2nd PUC Exam 2026, students need to secure at least 30% marks in each subject (considering both theory and practical components separately) and an aggregate of 33% across all subjects (equivalent to 198 out of 600 marks) to pass.

✅ Minimum 30% marks in each subject (theory + practical if applicable).

✅ Overall aggregate of 33% across all subjects (198/600 marks).

✅ Theory and practical marks are checked independently.

The Karnataka School Examination and Assessment Board continues its dedication to fostering a robust and supportive educational environment. By regularly updating its curriculum, examination patterns, and student support systems, KSEAB aims to prepare students not just for examinations, but for successful futures. Students are advised to regularly visit the official KSEAB website (kseab.karnataka.gov.in) for the latest announcements, sample papers, and resources to aid in their preparation.
