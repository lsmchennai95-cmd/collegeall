CBSE Physics Answer Sheet Mismatch: A Deep Dive into the Growing Controversy

The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) is once again under the scanner, facing intense scrutiny over alleged discrepancies in its answer sheet evaluation process. A recent case involving a Class 12 student's Physics answer sheet has ignited a major online discussion, bringing to light wider concerns about the board's newly introduced On-Screen Marking (OSM) system and its impact on student futures.

The Vedant Shrivastava Case: A Viral Allegation

The controversy gained significant traction after Vedant Shrivastava, a CBSE Class 12 student, took to social media platform X (formerly Twitter) to voice a startling claim. Following unexpectedly low marks in Physics, Vedant applied for photocopies of his answer sheets through the CBSE re-evaluation process.

Upon receiving the scanned copy of his Physics answer sheet, he was "shattered" to find that it did not belong to him. He alleged that the handwriting, answers, and overall writing style in the uploaded sheet were "completely different" from his own. To support his claim, Vedant shared comparisons between the disputed Physics paper and his English and Computer Science answer sheets, along with his handwritten notes, clearly demonstrating a significant mismatch in handwriting and content.

"The handwriting style, letter formation, spacing, slant, sentence flow, everything is different. This is not a minor variation. It is completely different writing." - Vedant Shrivastava

This public outcry quickly went viral, prompting thousands of reactions and sparking similar claims from other students facing issues with their re-evaluated answer sheets.

The On-Screen Marking (OSM) System Under Scrutiny

At the heart of this burgeoning issue is CBSE's new On-Screen Marking (OSM) system, introduced for Class 12 board exam evaluations. The board claimed that this digital evaluation system would minimize human error and enhance transparency during re-evaluation by providing scanned copies of answer scripts.

However, the Vedant Shrivastava case, along with other complaints, has cast a shadow of doubt over the system's effectiveness and reliability. Students have reported a range of problems, including blurry scans, missing pages, portal crashes, payment glitches, and, most critically, mismatched answer sheets.

✅ Expected Benefits of OSM:

✅ Reduce human error

✅ Improve access to answer scripts for re-evaluation

✅ Enhance transparency in evaluation

❌ Reported Issues with OSM:

❌ Blurry scans

❌ Missing pages

❌ Portal crashes and payment glitches

❌ Mismatched answer sheets

CBSE's Response and Corrective Measures

Initially, there was a perceived silence from the board, but the overwhelming social media attention eventually prompted a response. CBSE acknowledged the discrepancy in Vedant's Physics answer sheet. The board's Joint Secretary (Coordination) communicated directly with Vedant, sending him the correct Physics answer book and confirming that his result would be revised accordingly.

Furthermore, CBSE has acknowledged similar complaints, including one regarding a Chemistry answer sheet, and has stated that it is taking such cases "on top priority." They are actively examining all matters seriously and have resolved some of the reported issues.

Navigating the Re-evaluation Process

For students who find themselves in a similar predicament, understanding the CBSE re-evaluation process is crucial:

1. Apply for Scanned Copies
This is the mandatory first step. Only students who obtain scanned copies of their answer books are eligible for further verification or re-evaluation. You can apply for photocopies online through the official CBSE website.
2. Verification of Marks
If you suspect issues like incorrect totaling or marks not being awarded for attempted questions, you can apply for verification of marks.
3. Re-evaluation of Answers
For concerns about the evaluation of specific answers or perceived under-marking, students can apply for re-evaluation of individual questions. It is important to clearly explain the issue and compare your answers with the official marking scheme.

While the board charges fees for these services (e.g., Rs 700 per subject for scanned copies, Rs 500 for verification, and Rs 100 per question for re-evaluation), the recent events underscore the importance of students exercising these options to ensure fair assessment.

Moving Forward: A Call for Robustness and Transparency

The CBSE Physics answer sheet mismatch incident serves as a critical reminder of the immense pressure and emotional toll board exams have on students. It highlights the urgent need for CBSE to ensure the robustness and transparency of its evaluation processes, especially with the implementation of new digital systems like OSM.

While the board's swift action in Vedant's case is a positive step, continuous vigilance and proactive measures are essential to prevent such errors from recurring and to uphold the trust of millions of students and their families in the examination system. Students and parents continue to demand greater clarity and accountability, hoping for a future where such discrepancies are a thing of the past.
